3. WHY BEHIND THE CHANGE
   - Locality of Reference: Keeps routes, controllers, services, repositories, and
     validation schemas for a single domain (e.g., GatePass or Bed Bookings) in one place.
     When implementing complex logic (like offline QR signing or 10-minute soft lock state
     transitions), all relevant code is co-located without jumping across 5 distant folders.
   - Domain Boundary Encapsulation: Subsystem-specific helpers (e.g., QR JWT minting in
     gatepass, or PDF receipt generators in payments) remain encapsulated within their
     respective module rather than cluttering shared generic utility folders.
   - Team & AI Collaboration: Minimizes merge conflicts by isolating feature work into
     dedicated domain directories.
   - Scalability & Maintainability: Enables straightforward refactoring, testing, and
     future migration of specific modules (e.g., extracting payments or guard scanner sync)
     without touching unrelated modules.

4. WHY NOT THE ALTERNATIVE(S)
   - Alternative 1: Traditional Layer-Based Architecture (controllers/, services/, routes/)
     * Why Rejected: In complex apps with 11+ distinct business subdomains, layer-based
       folders become massive dumping grounds (e.g., 20+ controllers in one folder).
       Developers must constantly switch between 4-5 different directory trees to make a
       single feature change.
   - Alternative 2: Monolithic Single-File / Minimalist Routing (all-in-one index.js)
     * Why Rejected: Violates separation of concerns and fails to provide concurrency-safe,
       maintainable code for high-throughput bed bookings and guard offline queues.
   - Alternative 3: Microservices Setup at Day 1
     * Why Rejected: SRS v2 specifies a single university deployment, 8 hostels, and a
       shared database. Microservices would introduce unnecessary network overhead,
       distributed transaction complexity, and deployment friction at this scale. A
       modular monolith in Node.js/Express is the ideal architectural fit.
